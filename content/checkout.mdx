Every payment has a hosted checkout page at its `checkout_url`, and every
[payment link](/payment-links) has one at its own. Send payers there, or bring checkout to
your own site with [the widget or the overlay](/embeds). Create the payment as usual, then send the
payer to its `checkout_url`:

## What payers see

On a link's page, payers choose how to pay:

- the price, and each asset the link takes with what it costs now. An asset without a
  [rate](/prices-and-rates) at the moment is shown, but can't be chosen.
- for an open amount, its presets and, if you allow it, an amount of their own between your
  minimum and maximum
- for donations, a name and a message if they like, or neither

A payment's page shows them how to pay it:

- the amount, and the price when it has one
- a QR code, and a button that opens their wallet. Both carry a payment URI in the network's
  standard: BIP-21 on Bitcoin, EIP-681 on the EVM networks and Solana Pay on Solana. TRON and
  Bittensor have no standard, so their codes hold the address.
- the address to copy
- when the payment expires

The page follows the payment as it moves, without a reload: each deposit as it confirms, what is
still due after a partial payment, and the paid or expired page at the end. A screen reader
announces each change.

## Languages

Checkout comes in ten languages: English, Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese, French, German, Italian,
Turkish, Russian, Simplified Chinese and Japanese. It opens in the language a `lang` parameter
asks for (`?lang=de`), then the one the payer chose before, then their browser's, and English
otherwise. Payers can switch at any time.

## Receipts

On a link with `receipts: true`, the paid page offers the payer a receipt by email, and so does the
page of a payment that closed partly paid. It is sent once the payment is final, in the payer's
language. It shows your display name, what they paid, the price when there is one, and the
transactions. Nowgate uses the address for nothing else, and deletes it 30 days after sending.

## Branding and redirects

A link's [branding](/api/payment-links) appears on its page and on its payments' pages: the display
name, an accent color and a logo. Checkout picks a text color for the accent that meets WCAG AA.

With `success_url`, the paid page has a button back to your site. With `cancel_url`, the link's page
has a way back for a payer who changes their mind.

## Tokens and limits

The token in a checkout URL is the page's only key. Anyone holding the URL can see what the page
shows: the payment's amount, address and status, and your display name. They can't see its
metadata, your fees or your other payments, and the token doesn't work on the merchant API. Keep
checkout URLs out of public places, as you would a receipt.

Checkout makes at most 20 payments an hour for one IP address, and a link has at most 50 open at
once.
Beyond that, checkout answers [`attempt_limit`](/errors/attempt_limit) with `Retry-After`. A
payer's browser that already has an open payment for the same choice gets that payment back, so a
reload or a double click doesn't make a second one. An inactive link answers
[`link_inactive`](/errors/link_inactive).
