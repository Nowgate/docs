---
title: Payouts
description: Pay your balance out to your own wallets, singly, in batches or on a schedule, with destinations that wait, limits and approvals.
---

What your customers pay stays in your balance, per asset, until you pay it out to a wallet of
yours. A payout sends an asset you hold, on its own network: Nowgate never converts one asset into
another. The [API reference](/api/payouts) lists every field.

Every call that moves money, or sets up its movement, needs a key with the `payouts` scope. Such a
key is always bound to an IP allowlist, so a key copied off your server is of no use elsewhere.

## Your balance

`GET /v1/balances` shows each asset's balance in three parts:

| Part        | Meaning                                                              |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `available` | Settled and free. Payouts and refunds draw on this alone.            |
| `pending`   | Credited on Bitcoin, but not yet at its settle depth.                |
| `reserved`  | Held for payouts and refunds under way.                              |

## Destinations

A destination is an address of yours on one network. Add one with
`POST /v1/payout-destinations`, or pay an address directly, which adds it.

- **A new destination is paid within about a minute,** once Nowgate's signer has registered it.
  `destination.created` is sent at once, so your systems can raise an alarm about an address
  nobody meant to add, and `destination.active` once it can be paid.
- **Addresses are checked** before they are taken: each network's format and checksum, and never
  one of Nowgate's own addresses. On TRON give the `T…` form.
- **Disabling** one (`DELETE /v1/payout-destinations/{id}`) stops it at once. Payouts still waiting
  for it fail, and what they held comes back to your balance. Adding the address again makes a
  new destination.
- You can have up to 1,000 enabled destinations.

## Making a payout

<SdkTabs region="payout">

```bash
curl https://api.nowgate.io/v1/payouts \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $NOWGATE_PAYOUTS_KEY" \
  -H "Idempotency-Key: $(uuidgen)" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"network":"tron","asset":"USDT","address":"T...","amount":"250","external_id":"pay-run-42"}'
```

</SdkTabs>

| Field                                          | Meaning                                                                                               |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `destination`, or `network`, `address`         | Where it goes.                                                                                        |
| `asset`, `amount`                              | What, as a decimal string in the asset's units.                                                       |
| `fee_paid_by`                                  | `merchant` (the default): the destination gets `amount`. `payee`: the network fee comes out of it.     |
| `external_id`                                  | Your own reference, unique among your payouts.                                                        |
| `send_at`                                      | Optional: send no earlier than this, at most 30 days ahead.                                           |
| `webhook_url`, `metadata`                      | As on payments: this payout's events go to its own URL, signed with your default endpoint's secret.   |

The payout's whole cost is reserved from your available balance at once. `amount_debited` is what
it takes from your balance, and `amount_sent` what the destination receives.

### Fees

- **The network fee** is quoted in the payout's own asset when you create it, as the most the
  network can take, and reserved with the amount. Once the payout is finished, `network_fee` is
  what the network took and the rest is back in your balance: `amount_debited` then says what
  the payout cost you.
- **Nowgate's payout fee** is `payout_fee`, 0.
- When the network's fee can't be estimated right now, or a token can't be priced, the payout is
  refused with `fee_estimate_unavailable` or `rate_unavailable` (`503`). Retry shortly, with the
  same `Idempotency-Key`.

## Statuses

| Status                | Meaning                                                                                                 |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `awaiting_approval`   | Your account requires a person's approval, and nobody has given it yet.                                 |
| `scheduled`           | `send_at` is still ahead.                                                                               |
| `waiting_destination` | Its destination is new, and Nowgate's signer is registering it: within about a minute.                  |
| `queued`              | Ready, and about to be sent. `waiting_reason` says why when it waits longer than usual.                 |
| `sending`             | Signed and broadcast; `transaction` shows it. Not final yet.                                            |
| `finished`            | Final on the chain.                                                                                     |
| `failed`              | It couldn't be sent, or the network refused it. `failure_reason` says why, and its cost is back in your balance. |
| `canceled`            | You canceled it before it was signed, and its cost is back in your balance.                             |

Each payout sends `payout.created`, then `payout.<status>` on every change, to your
[webhooks](/webhooks). Cancel one with `POST /v1/payouts/{id}/cancel` until it is signed; once it
is `sending`, it can't be stopped.

## Batches and CSV

`POST /v1/payout-batches` makes up to 1,000 payouts at once, as JSON `items`, or as CSV text in a
`csv` field (UTF-8, at most 256 KiB) whose header names its columns:

```csv
network,asset,address,amount,fee_paid_by,external_id
tron,USDT,T...,120.50,,inv-1001
solana,USDC,7Ec...,75,payee,inv-1002
```

A batch is all or nothing. Every item is checked first, and one bad item refuses the whole batch,
with every problem listed by item (`/items/3/amount`) or by CSV line (`/csv/line/4/address`).
Each item is an ordinary payout with its own ID and events; the batch sends `batch.created`, and
`batch.finished` once every item is final.

## Limits

Each asset has a limit per payout and per day (payouts created in a rolling 24 hours), about
10,000 USD and 50,000 USD of the asset by default. Batches, refunds and forwarding count too. A
payout over a limit is refused with `payout_limit_exceeded`, which says what remains.

## Approvals

Your account can require a person's approval for every payout and refund: each then waits in
`awaiting_approval` until a member of your team approves it in the dashboard, with their second
factor bound to exactly what they approve. No API key can approve one, whatever its scopes: that is what
makes it a second step. Under `two_person`, the approver must be someone other than the payout's
creator. See [payout approvals](/approvals).

## In the sandbox

The sandbox's payouts are simulated. They go through the same API, limits, reservations, statuses
and webhooks, but no chain: a payout is `sending` at once, with a `sim:` transaction hash, and
`finished` about 10 seconds later. Its network fee is a fixed, notional amount.

- [`POST /v1/sandbox/payout-destinations/{id}/activate`](/api/sandbox) makes a new destination
  usable at once, instead of after 5 minutes.
- [`POST /v1/sandbox/payouts/{id}/simulate`](/api/sandbox) with `action: "fail"` fails a payout
  that hasn't finished.
- The sandbox's balance is its simulated deposits: a payout of a balance that came from a real
  testnet deposit fails with `simulated_funds_insufficient`.
